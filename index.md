<p>
I am a political researcher based in Houston, Texas. I earned my Joint Bachelor’s degree in Government and African-American Studies from Harvard University in 2026, graduating cum laude with a minor in Computer Science and a Data Science research citation. I currently work as a data scientist and field director for a political strategy firm organizing campaigns and conducting economic development and public opinion research. 
</p>
<p>
I use data to solve public policy and legal problems, bringing a computational perspective to a diverse range of contemporary social issues. I’ve always been fascinated by the intersection of race and society and have used data to understand our modern political and cultural systems, including policing, surveillance technologies, social media, and education.
</p>
<p>
As one of the first undergraduates in my program’s 50-year history to combine Black Studies and Computer Science, I hope to use technology to bridge the gap between Black Studies and quantitative political science and expand the field of legal empirics.
</p>
<p>
While at Harvard, I served as a Teaching Fellow in the Department of Statistics, a Data Specialist and Teaching Assistant in the Department of African and African-American Studies, and a cross-departmental Research Assistant in the Division of Social Science. I am currently the Kwame Anthony Appiah Prize Fellow for my empirical work on racial disparity in stop-and-frisk policing.
</p>
<p>
I hope to pursue graduate studies, namely joint JD/PhD programs, and continue my training. In my career, I aim to use empirics to develop people-centered policies and build a more equitable global future online and in-person.
</p>
